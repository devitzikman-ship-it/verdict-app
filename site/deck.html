VERDICT 04 / 10
The Critical Question
How many users actually pass?
This is what makes or breaks the business. Prediction markets are essentially binary options with ~50/50 odds. With 2% slippage on every trade, the house edge is baked in.
Why most users fail
  • Need +10% to pass, but only -10% to fail (asymmetric)
  • 2% slippage on EVERY buy and sell = 4% round-trip cost
  • Must trade 3+ days in 14 days (forces activity)
  • 25% max position size prevents one lucky bet
  • Prediction markets are efficient - edges are rare
  • Most retail traders lose money (industry-wide ~75-85%)
Expected Pass Rates
Prop firm industry average4-10%
Topstep reported pass rate~6%
FTMO reported pass rate~10%
With 4% round-trip slippage~5-8%
Conservative estimate: 7% pass rate
70 of 1,000 users pass eval each month. This is generous - with binary outcome markets + slippage, it could be lower.

VERDICT 07 / 10
Your Built-In Edge
The 2% slippage is your house edge
Every time a user buys, they pay 2% above market. Every time they sell, they receive 2% below market. This is the engine that keeps pass rates low.
4%
Round-Trip Cost
User buys at +2%, sells at -2%. Even if the price doesn't move, they lose 4% on every position.
2.5 trades
Trades to Hit Max Loss
A user going all-in each time (25% position cap) loses ~4% per round trip. After ~2.5 full cycles, they've lost 10% and fail.
14 days
Time Pressure
Users must hit +10% in 14 days. Short timeline + slippage + forced 3-day minimum trading = most expire or bust.
Comparison: Casino house edge
Blackjack: 0.5% edge. Roulette: 2.7%. Slots: 5-15%. Your 4% round-trip slippage puts VERDICT's house edge between roulette and slots, but with the perception of skill-based trading. This is exactly how prop firms work.
VERDICT 08 / 10
Legal Analysis
Is this legal?
Why it should be fine
  • No real trading occurs. You are not a broker, exchange, or market maker. Trades are simulated against live price feeds.
  • Subscription model. Users pay for access to a platform/tool, like a trading simulator with prizes. Similar to DraftKings for trading.
  • Precedent exists. Topstep, FTMO, Apex Trader, MyForexFunds all operate this model. Combined they do $500M+/year.
  • Not securities. You're not selling investment contracts. Users buy access to a skill evaluation.
  • Payouts are prizes. Framed as performance bonuses, not investment returns. Similar to contest winnings.
Risks to watch
  • Gambling classification. Some states/countries may classify this as gambling since outcomes are binary. Prediction markets themselves sit in a legal gray area in the US.
  • MyForexFunds precedent. CFTC shut down MFF in Aug 2023 for fraud (misrepresenting how capital was used). You must be transparent that trades are simulated.
  • State-by-state rules. Some US states have stricter contest/sweepstakes laws. May need to exclude certain states.
  • Polymarket's own status. Polymarket paid a $1.4M CFTC settlement. If they face more issues, your price feed could be affected.
  • Tax reporting. Payouts over $600/year require 1099 reporting to the IRS.
Recommendation
Get a lawyer who specializes in fintech/gaming to review your Terms of Service. Budget $2-5K for this. The key legal points: (1) clearly state trades are simulated, (2) frame payouts as performance bonuses, (3) add arbitration clause, (4) exclude restricted jurisdictions. This is NOT optional before launch.
